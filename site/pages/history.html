History & Lineage

How NAGP began, and the roots of Gestalt therapy.

How NAGP began

NAGP grew out of two national gatherings of Gestalt practitioners, and the work of many volunteers in between.

  1. April 5 and 6, 2025

    The inaugural gathering in Chicago

    Gestalt practitioners met at The Westin O'Hare in Rosemont, Illinois, in person and on Zoom. Together they adopted a name, the National Association of Gestalt Practitioners, set values and a sense of mission, and drafted an initial purpose. They also formed a Next Steps Group to shepherd the development of the association.

    Donations from the community helped make the gathering possible.

  2. April 24 to 26, 2026

    The second national gathering in Denver

    The community met again at the Gestalt Institute of the Rockies in Denver, Colorado. The program covered NAGP's mission, a presentation on the legal formation of the organization, proposed motions and a vote. Work groups explored the culture of the organization and the principles that would guide the legal entity.

  3. Mid-2026

    The Founding Council begins

    A Founding Council of five members began meeting to establish NAGP's structure, governance and programming. The Council opened a bank account and began financial operations.

  4. September 27, 2026

    The first open community meeting

    The Founding Council held its first open community meeting with Gestalt practitioners from across the United States. The community Google Group mailing list now connects about 150 or more people.

  5. Coming next

    Membership and the first programs

    The Council is preparing to launch membership in the coming months, along with salons and presentations.

The roots of Gestalt therapy

NAGP stands in a lineage that reaches back more than seventy years. Here is a short outline.

From Europe to South Africa

Frederick (Fritz) Perls and Laura Perls were German psychoanalysts. Laura had studied Gestalt psychology, and both were influenced by the neurologist Kurt Goldstein. They left Germany in 1933 and, after a time in the Netherlands, settled in South Africa. There Fritz wrote Ego, Hunger and Aggression, with contributions from Laura. The book's subtitle was “A Revision of Freud's Theory and Method.” It marked their move away from classical psychoanalysis.

New York: the founding years

The Perlses moved to New York in the late 1940s. There they worked with the writer and social critic Paul Goodman and others. In 1951, Frederick Perls, Ralph Hefferline and Paul Goodman published Gestalt Therapy: Excitement and Growth in the Human Personality, the founding text of the approach.

In 1952 the New York Institute for Gestalt Therapy was founded. Laura Perls taught and led there for decades, and teachers such as Isadore From trained generations of practitioners.

Cleveland

The Gestalt Institute of Cleveland was founded in 1954. It became a major center for training, and for bringing Gestalt ideas to groups, organizations and communities. Teachers connected with Cleveland include Erving and Miriam Polster, Joseph Zinker, and Edwin and Sonia Nevis.

Esalen and the 1960s

In the 1960s Fritz Perls taught at the Esalen Institute in Big Sur, California. His workshops and books, such as Gestalt Therapy Verbatim (1969), brought Gestalt therapy to a wide public. He later started a Gestalt community at Lake Cowichan on Vancouver Island, British Columbia. He died in 1970.

Growth across the continent

In the following decades, Gestalt training institutes opened in many cities across the United States and Canada. Practitioners and writers deepened the theory, with growing attention to dialogue, the relationship and the field. In 1989 the Association for the Advancement of Gestalt Therapy (AAGT) was founded as an international community. It is now called the International Association for the Advancement of Gestalt Therapy (IAAGT).
